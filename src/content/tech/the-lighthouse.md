My grandfather spent 47 years as a lighthouse keeper on the Faroe Islands.

Every winter, ships would radio in asking if the light was finally being replaced with the new automated system.

Every winter, he'd say yes, absolutely, the decommissioning is scheduled for Q2.

He retired in 1987. The light is still there.

I think about him every time our enterprise project managers hold the quarterly all-hands to announce that KRONOS — our 34-year-old claims processing application running on a server that requires a specific discontinued brand of compressed air to clean — is finally, definitively, irreversibly being sunset by March.

That was eleven Marches ago.

Last week I found the original decommissioning slide deck from 2013. The font was Calibri. The confidence was extraordinary.

KRONOS has now outlasted three "replacement platforms," two CIOs, one office building, and a vendor who entered witness protection.

My therapist says I have "deadline grief."

She's billing it under a new diagnostic code she invented specifically for me.

The project managers are currently in a room right now drawing a new roadmap. I can hear the markers squeaking.

The light keeps spinning.
